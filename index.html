Project Overview

Many useful recombinant proteins are difficult to express or use because they misfold, aggregate, or lose activity. This creates problems for protein production, storage, and application in biotechnology.

Our project explores whether intrinsically disordered protein, or IDP, tags can help target proteins remain soluble and functional. We are combining computational design with experimental selection to evolve better tags.

Our Rationale

IDPs are flexible proteins that do not adopt one fixed structure. This flexibility may help them act as solubility tags, protective partners, or stress-tolerance elements.

Instead of redesigning each difficult protein individually, we want to test whether short transferable tags can improve protein behaviour across different systems.

IDPs prove an ideal candidate for solubility tags as they are typically small (about 60 amino acids), and thus often do not significantly interfere with the function of the target protein. Additionally, IDPs are highly soluble and flexible, which allows them to act as a protective partner for the target protein, preventing aggregation and misfolding. In fact, many natural tags involved in protein solubility and stress tolerance are intrinsically disordered, such as the N-terminal domain of Hsp70 chaperones involved in protein homeostasis in human cells.

Project Goals

  • Design and generate libraries of candidate IDP tags.
  • Test whether these tags improve target protein solubility.
  • Identify sequence features linked to improved solubility or stability.
  • Validate selected sequences experimentally with wide range of target proteins.
  • Document our workflow for future work by the directed evolution community.

Experimental Plan

Our main experimental plan involves computationally generating mutant sequences from a set of IDP templates, and determining mutation hotspots to select the best potential candidates to form IDP libraries. Each mutant sequence was computationally analysed and scored for solubility through traits such as GRAVY score and solubility fraction.The best candidates were condensed into a single degenerate DNA sequence which we used to procure a mutant library through the Ultramer Oligo serveice from Integrated DNA Technologies (IDT).

To provide a baseline for determining the effectiveness of computationally selecting for mutants, we simultaneously performed error prone PCR on the template sequences to obtain libraries of completely random mutants. All of the libraries generated were cloned into a vector containing an aggregation prone form of b-lactamase for carbenicillin selection. The cells containing the best performing tags could then survive carbenicillin selection, and be subsequently extracted and anaylsed.

Why Directed Evolution?

IDPs do not have fixed conformations and thus their behaviour under physiological conditions are often difficult to predict by rational models. However, their mechanisms in promoting protein solubility have been characterised by prior works, giving us a strong foundation to build upon. We are using directed evolution to explore the sequence space of IDPs and identify the sequence features that are most important for solubility and stability. This will allow us to design better IDP tags for applications in biotechnology and therapeutics.
